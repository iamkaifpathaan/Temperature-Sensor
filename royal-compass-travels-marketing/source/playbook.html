Website SEO

SEO (Search Engine Optimization) means improving a website so that search engines like Google can find it, understand it, and consider it a good result for relevant searches.

The concepts, in plain language

TermWhat it meansTravel example
SEOEverything we do so the website shows up (and is clicked) for relevant Google searches without paying for ads.Someone searches "Dubai tour package from Mumbai" and finds Royal Compass.
On-page SEOWhat is on each page: titles, descriptions, headings, text, images, links. Making each page clearly about one topic.The Bali page has a clear title, a proper heading, helpful text about the trip and a clear enquiry button.
Technical SEOThe "plumbing": can Google access and load the pages? Is the site fast enough, mobile-friendly, secure (HTTPS), free of broken links, with a sitemap?A package page that Google cannot access because of a setting will never appear in search, however good its content.
KeywordThe words people type into Google. We pick realistic ones that match what the client actually sells."Kerala honeymoon package", "Thailand visa for Indians", "family tour package Europe".
Page title (title tag)The blue clickable headline shown in Google results. Around 50–60 characters.Kerala Honeymoon Packages | Royal Compass Travels
Meta descriptionThe short grey summary under the title in Google. It does not directly improve ranking, but a good one gets more clicks.Customised 5–7 day Kerala honeymoon trips with houseboat stays. Share your dates and get a quote.
Heading structureOne main heading (H1) per page, then sub-headings (H2, H3) in a logical order — like chapters in a book.H1: Kerala Honeymoon Packages → H2: What's included → H2: Best time to visit → H2: FAQs
Internal linkingLinks from one page of the site to another. Helps visitors and Google find related pages.The "Visa help" page links to the relevant destination packages.
IndexingGoogle storing a page in its database. A page that is not indexed cannot appear in results.We check that each important page is actually indexed.
Google Search ConsoleA free Google tool that shows how the site appears in Google: which searches it shows up for, clicks, indexing problems, and errors.We can see that the site appeared 300 times for "Goa trip package" and got 6 clicks.

What we will actually do

Review & setup

  • Basic technical & on-page review of the existing site
  • Google Search Console setup and verification
  • Sitemap submission (if the site has or can generate one)
  • Basic indexing / visibility checks on key pages
  • Record a baseline so we can compare later

Improvements

  • Rewrite page titles and meta descriptions
  • Fix heading structure (one H1, logical H2/H3s)
  • Add relevant travel/service keywords naturally
  • Add useful internal links
  • Improve thin or unclear content where needed
  • Monitor early search data in Search Console

Why SEO takes time

Google has to re-visit ("crawl") the updated pages, re-evaluate them, and compare them against every other travel website targeting the same searches — including large online travel portals with years of history. That process usually takes weeks to months, not days. Month 1 is about fixing the foundation so that improvement is possible; it is not when rankings usually move much.

What we CAN controlWhat we CANNOT control
  • Quality and clarity of on-page content
  • Titles, descriptions, headings, internal links
  • Fixing technical issues we find (within scope)
  • Search Console setup and sitemap submission
  • Choosing sensible, realistic keywords
  • Honest monitoring and reporting
  • Google's algorithm and how often it changes
  • How fast Google re-crawls and re-ranks pages
  • What competitors do and how strong their sites are
  • Domain history, age and existing reputation
  • How many people search for a given term
  • Hosting/server issues outside our access
Say this — "When will I rank?"

Honestly, nobody can give you a fixed date — Google decides rankings, and it looks at hundreds of factors including your competitors. What we can do this month is fix the basics so Google can read your site properly, set up Search Console so we can actually see how you're appearing, and start tracking it. Usually you start seeing early movement in impressions over a few weeks to a few months. We'll show you the real numbers every month so you can see the trend yourself.

Don't say this
  • "You'll be on page 1 in 30 days." / "We'll get you to #1 on Google."
  • "SEO will bring you X visitors or X leads."
  • "We guarantee ranking for [keyword]."

GEO — Generative Engine Optimization

GEO means making a website's information clear, accurate and well-structured so that AI-based tools (like ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews) can understand it correctly if they use it when answering someone's question.

What it means, simply

More people now ask AI tools things like "Which travel agencies in [city] do customised Dubai trips?" These tools build answers from information available on the web. If a business's website is vague, inconsistent or hard to read, the AI has less to work with. GEO is about removing that confusion: clear facts, clear structure, consistent information.

Why it is still an evolving area

  • Each AI tool works differently, and the companies behind them do not publish exact rules for which sources they use.
  • Their behaviour changes often — sometimes month to month.
  • There is no reliable "GEO ranking" report like Search Console for Google. Measurement is limited.
  • Much of what helps GEO is the same as good SEO and good content: clarity, accuracy, trust signals.

What we will actually do

TaskExample for Royal Compass
Clear "about" informationWho they are, where they are based, how long they've operated (only if the client confirms), what kind of trips they arrange, who they serve.
Clear service descriptionsSeparate, plainly worded explanations of each service they actually offer (e.g. holiday packages, flights, visa assistance — only what the client confirms).
Clear destination informationEach destination page states what the trip includes, typical duration, best time to go, who it suits.
Direct answers / FAQsShort, factual answers to common questions (overlaps with AEO — see Section 3).
Consistent business detailsSame name, address, phone and email everywhere on the site (and ideally on Instagram / Google profile).
Structured data (where feasible)Adding standard "schema" code that labels information for machines — e.g. "this is a travel agency", "this is an FAQ". Only where the website platform allows it without major development.
Say this — explaining GEO to the client

More people are now asking AI tools like ChatGPT or Google's AI answers for travel suggestions. Those tools read information from websites. We're going to make sure your website explains clearly who you are, what you offer and where you take people — in a structured way that's easy for these systems to understand. Nobody can control whether an AI tool mentions a specific business, but a clear, well-organised website gives you a better chance than a vague one.

Don't say this
  • "Your website will appear in ChatGPT / Gemini / Perplexity."
  • "We'll get AI to recommend you."
  • "GEO is the new SEO that gets you results faster."

AEO — Answer Engine Optimization

AEO means writing content that directly answers the specific questions people ask — so the page is genuinely useful when someone searches a question, and so search engines and voice/AI assistants can easily pick out the answer.

What we will actually do

  1. Collect real questions. Ask the client what customers ask most on calls and WhatsApp. Also look at Google's "People also ask" boxes and search suggestions.
  2. Write short, direct answers. Answer in the first 1–2 sentences, then add detail.
  3. Structure them clearly. Use the question as a heading, the answer right below it, and FAQ sections on relevant pages.
  4. Improve informational pages. Make sure destination and service pages actually answer what a traveller wants to know before enquiring.

Example questions for a travel agency

Planning

  • What is the best time to visit Bali?
  • How many days are enough for Dubai?
  • Is Thailand good for a family trip with kids?

Booking & service

  • Can you customise a package?
  • What is included in the package price?
  • Do you help with visas? (only if they do)
  • What is your cancellation policy?
Example — weak vs good answer

Weak: "Bali is a beautiful destination loved by travellers from all over the world, offering many experiences throughout the year…"

Good: "The best time to visit Bali is generally April to October, which is the dry season. July and August are the busiest months, so prices are usually higher. November to March is the wet season, with more rain but fewer crowds."

Facts like seasons and visa rules must be checked and, for anything the client sells, confirmed by the client. Never publish visa or price information we have not verified.

SEO vs GEO vs AEO — the difference

SEOGEOAEO
Main goalAppear in Google search results and get clicksBe clearly understood by AI tools that generate answersDirectly answer the questions people ask
FocusPages, keywords, technical healthClarity, facts, structure, consistencyQuestions and short, clear answers
Travel example"Kerala tour package" page is well-builtSite clearly states who Royal Compass is and what it offers"Do I need a visa for Dubai?" answered in 2 sentences

In practice, they overlap a lot. The same clean, well-structured, helpful page supports all three. It is fine to tell the client that honestly.

Say this — "Does AEO guarantee featured snippets or AI answers?"

No — and anyone who says it does isn't being straight with you. Google and AI tools choose which answers to show, and they change how they do it regularly. What AEO does is make your pages the kind of clear, direct answer those systems tend to prefer. It improves your chances and it helps real visitors too, but it's not something anyone can guarantee.

Don't say this
  • "We'll get you into the featured snippet / answer box."
  • "Your FAQs will show up in Google's AI answers."

Meta Ads & lead generation

Meta Ads are paid ads that run on Facebook and Instagram. We will use the client's ₹10,000 budget to test which ads and audiences bring the most relevant travel enquiries.

Creative vs campaign

Ad creative

What people actually see: the image or video, the headline, the text, and the button (e.g. "Get Quote"). We are making 5 of these.

Ad campaign

The container and settings: what the goal is (leads, traffic), who sees the ads, how much is spent, and for how long. Creatives live inside a campaign.

How Meta Ads Manager is organised

Meta Ads Manager is the tool (inside the client's Meta Business account) where ads are created, budgets are set, and results are tracked. It has three levels:

CampaignThe goal. For us: Leads. One campaign is enough for Month 1.
Ad setThe audience, budget, schedule and placements. E.g. "Mumbai, 25–50, interested in international travel".
AdThe creative people see. Several ads can sit in one ad set.

Key terms

TermPlain meaningExample
AudienceThe group of people the ad is shown to.Adults in Delhi NCR interested in travel
TargetingThe settings that define the audience: location, age, gender, interests, language.Location: Pune; Age: 28–55; Interest: Holidays
ImpressionsHow many times the ad was shown in total (one person can see it several times).20,000 impressions
ReachHow many different people saw the ad at least once.8,000 people
FrequencyImpressions ÷ reach — average times each person saw it.2.5
CPMCost per 1,000 impressions — how "expensive" the audience is to reach.₹120 per 1,000 views
ClicksHow many times people clicked the ad (use link clicks, not all clicks).180 link clicks
CTRClick-through rate = clicks ÷ impressions × 100. Shows how interesting the ad is.180 ÷ 20,000 = 0.9%
CPCCost per click = spend ÷ clicks.₹2,400 ÷ 180 = ₹13.3
LeadA person who shares their contact details and shows interest — e.g. fills a form with name, phone and destination.12 leads
Cost per lead (CPL)Spend ÷ leads.₹2,400 ÷ 12 = ₹200

All numbers above are made up to show the maths. They are not benchmarks and must never be quoted to the client as expected results.

Traffic vs lead generation

A traffic campaign asks Meta to find people likely to click. A lead generation campaign asks Meta to find people likely to submit their details. Clicks are cheap and easy; enquiries are what the client needs. We will run a leads objective — either Meta's instant form (the form opens inside Facebook/Instagram) or the website enquiry form. Decide this with the client before launch:

Meta instant formWebsite enquiry form
ProsQuick for the user, usually more leads for the moneyLeads land in the client's admin area; visitor sees the website first, often more serious
ConsCan bring lower-intent leads; leads sit in Meta (client downloads them from Meta Leads Center / Business Suite)Usually fewer leads; depends on site speed and form quality
Must haveA privacy policy URL (Meta requires it) and 1–2 qualifying questions (destination, travel month)Working form, fast mobile page, tracking set up
Note

Instant-form leads do not automatically go into the website admin area. Connecting them is not in our Month 1 scope. The client (or we, if agreed) downloads them from Meta. Make sure the client knows where to find these leads and checks them daily.

₹10,000 is a testing budget

₹10,000 is a small budget for paid ads. That is fine — but it means the goal of Month 1 is to learn, not to scale. With this budget, each ad gets a limited amount of spend, so results are directional ("this looks better") rather than statistically certain. Meta's system also needs time and data to "learn" who responds best, and small budgets give it less data.

Suggested way to use the budget (guideline, adjust to real data)

PhaseApprox. daysApprox. spendWhat happens
1. TestFirst 5–7 days of running~₹4,000All 5 creatives run with an equal chance. We watch CTR, CPC, leads and lead quality.

How to choose the "best 3" ads

Decide in this order. Do not pick an ad just because it got the most likes.

  1. Relevant leads — did it bring enquiries that the client says are genuine (real travel interest, reachable phone)?
  2. Cost per lead — among ads with leads, which cost less per lead?
  3. CTR and CPC — if no ad has enough leads yet, which gets the most interest per rupee?
  4. Enough spend to judge — don't kill an ad after ₹100. Give each a fair share before deciding.

Write down why each ad was paused or kept. The client will ask.

If ads perform poorly

  • Check the basics first: is the ad approved, is the form working, is the audience too small or too broad?
  • Look at where the drop happens: low CTR = creative/message not interesting; good CTR but no leads = form, landing page or offer problem; leads but poor quality = targeting or form too easy.
  • Change one thing at a time (creative, audience, or form) so we know what made the difference.
  • Tell the client early and plainly. Poor early results are useful data — hiding them is what damages trust.

Likely questions about ads

"How many leads will ₹10,000 generate?"
Say
I can't give you a fixed number, and I'd be suspicious of anyone who does. It depends on the destination, the offer, the audience and competition. What this ₹10,000 will tell us is roughly what a lead costs for your business and which ads work best — that's what we need before spending more.
"Can you guarantee leads?"
Say
No, we can't guarantee leads — Meta decides who sees the ads, and people decide whether to enquire. What we guarantee is the work: 5 well-made ads, a proper test, active monitoring, and a clear report of what happened and why.
"Why didn't we get leads?"
Say
Let me show you where it stopped. If people saw the ad but didn't click, the ad or offer wasn't strong enough for that audience. If they clicked but didn't fill the form, the form or page needs work. Here's what we're changing next based on that.
"Why is the CPC high?"
Say
CPC goes up when the audience is competitive — travel is — or when the ad isn't getting many clicks for how often it's shown. We're watching it, testing which creative gets cheaper clicks, and we'll shift budget toward those. Also, a low CPC isn't the goal on its own — the cost per genuine enquiry is.
"Why are people clicking but not enquiring?"
Say
That usually means the ad is interesting but something after the click is stopping them — the form might be too long, the page slow on mobile, the price or offer unclear, or they're just browsing. We'll check each of these and fix what's in our control.
"Should we increase the budget?"
Say
Not yet. Let's first finish this test and see the cost per enquiry and the lead quality. If an ad is clearly bringing good enquiries at a cost that makes sense for your margins, then increasing gradually makes sense. Putting more money into something unproven usually just costs more.
"Which country should we target?"
Say
Where are most of your current customers from, and where can you actually serve and collect payment? We'd start there. For a small test budget, it's better to focus on one or two locations than spread thin.
"Can we target the US and UK?"
Say
Technically yes. But ads in the US and UK usually cost much more per view and per click than in India, so ₹10,000 would go much less far. If you have a specific offer for NRIs or travellers there and you can serve them, we can discuss a small separate test — but for Month 1 I'd suggest starting where your customers already are.
"Why did one ad perform better than another?"
Say
Usually it's the first second — the image or hook — and how clearly the offer is stated. Some destinations also simply attract more interest. We'll show you the ads side by side with their numbers, and use what worked to guide the next creatives.
Don't say this
  • "₹10,000 will get you around 50 leads." (or any number)
  • "Leads will be cheaper than on Google."
  • "We'll target only rich people / only people ready to book."
  • "All 5 ads will run the whole month."

Website fixes & lead funnel

We will fix what is getting in the way of enquiries, add essential legal pages, connect Search Console, and build a protected admin area where website enquiries land — owned and controlled by the client.

Legal pages — why they matter

  • Trust: travellers are paying significant amounts; clear policies reassure them.
  • Platform requirements: Meta lead forms require a privacy policy link. Payment gateways usually require terms and refund policies.
  • Data handling: the site collects names, phone numbers and emails, so it should explain how that data is used. India's data protection law (DPDP Act, 2023) makes this more important.

Typical pages: Privacy Policy, Terms & Conditions, Cancellation & Refund Policy, and optionally a Disclaimer.

Note — we are not lawyers

We will draft these from standard structures using the client's actual policies (cancellation rules, refund timelines, etc.). The client must review and approve them, and we should recommend they have a legal professional check them. Never invent refund or cancellation terms.

Search Console: verification and indexing

  • Verification proves to Google that the client owns the site. Usually done by adding a small code (DNS record at the domain provider, or an HTML tag/file on the site). The Search Console property should be owned by the client's Google account, with us added as a user.
  • Sitemap — a list of the site's pages we submit so Google can find them.
  • Indexing — whether Google has stored a page. Search Console shows which pages are indexed and why some are not.
  • Why data takes time: after setup, Search Console typically starts showing data after a few days, and it becomes useful for spotting trends after a few weeks. In Month 1 we mostly get a baseline.

Why client-controlled credentials matter

  • The leads are the client's customers' personal data. They should own and control access.
  • It protects us: if data is ever misused, it is clear we did not have access.
  • It avoids dependency: the client is never locked out because of us.
  • In practice: we set up the area; the client sets the password on first login (or via a reset link). We never ask for, store, or share it.
Note — basic security we should include

HTTPS on the site, spam protection on the form (e.g. a hidden honeypot field or CAPTCHA), a strong-password requirement, and an email notification to the client for each new enquiry. Confirm where the data is stored and that it is backed up with the hosting.

Why client delays delay results

Everything in Month 1 is chained together. For example: no Meta access → ads can't be set up → no test data by month-end. No approval of creatives → ads launch late → less time to learn. To make the most of this month, we'll need a few things from your side early — access to accounts, some photos and package details, and quick approvals. Anything that comes in late will push that part of the plan back, so we'll send you one simple list on day one and keep reminding you gently.

Metrics that matter

  • Leads and cost per lead
  • Lead quality (from client feedback)
  • Search clicks and queries
  • Profile visits, link taps, DMs

Vanity metrics (context only)

  • Impressions and reach on their own
  • Likes
  • Follower count
  • Cheap clicks that don't lead to enquiries

Not useless — but never present them as "results".

Month-end report outline

  1. Summary — 4–5 lines: what was done, key numbers, main learning.
  2. Work completed — checklist against the agreed scope.
  3. Website — Search Console numbers, what changed, what's pending.
  4. Instagram — output delivered, top 3 posts/reels, reach, profile visits.
  5. Meta Ads — spend, leads, CPL, which ads were kept/paused and why.
  6. Leads — total enquiries (website + Meta), quality as reported by client.
  7. What we learned — honest, including what didn't work.
  8. Recommendation — continue / adjust / pause, with reasons.
Note — attribution honesty

Don't claim credit for bookings we can't trace. If a customer called after seeing Instagram, only the client knows that. Report what the data shows, and ask the client what they observed.

Handling scope creep politely

Scope creep is when extra work slowly gets added without anyone agreeing to it ("Can you just also…"). It is normal and usually not bad intent. Handle it early and kindly.

  1. Don't say yes on the spot. "Good idea — let me check how that fits into this month's plan and get back to you today."
  2. Check it against the scope (Section 0). Is it clearly included?
  3. If it's small and reasonable, you may do it once as goodwill — but say so: "We'll include this one, it's a small one."
  4. If it's not small, offer options: swap it for something in scope, add it to next month, or quote it separately.
  5. Confirm in writing — a short WhatsApp/email message is enough.
Say this — examples

A Google Ads campaign is a great idea for later, but it isn't part of this month's plan. We could look at it once we have the Meta results — want me to note it for the month-end review?

Building a full new booking page is more than a fix — I'd rather do it properly. I'll send you a separate short quote so you can decide.

We can definitely replace one of this week's posts with the Diwali offer, so it stays within the 30 posts.
